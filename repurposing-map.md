# Repurposing Map

One long video is not one piece of content. It is the source material for a week of content — if you extract it deliberately.

The rule: **research and record once, distribute in every format that fits the idea.** Never record a separate, lesser version for each platform when the long-form source already contains it.

## The Map

Starting point: **1 long-form, horizontal video** (scripted with `scripting-system.md`, packaged with `packaging-checklist.md`).

| Output | Source in the long video | Format | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shorts / Reels / TikTok cuts | 1 strong moment per cut: a myth call-out, a single tip, the hook reworked, a surprising line | Vertical, short-form | See vertical rules below. Each cut stands alone — no "part 3, go watch the channel" with no payoff |
| Quote posts | 1–3 quotable lines from the script | Text post / image post (X, Threads, LinkedIn, Facebook) | Use the exact script wording where it is strong; add brief context, not a sales pitch |
| Carousel | The video's section structure | Multi-slide carousel (Instagram / LinkedIn) | One section per slide group: cover = the promise, middle = the sections, final = summary + one next step |
| Community post | The video's central question or poll angle | YouTube Community / channel post | Ask the question the video answers; link or reference the video for the full breakdown |
| Blog outline | Full script, restructured | Written outline → article draft | Headings from sections, hook becomes the intro, CTA becomes the conclusion. Expand examples in writing; do not paste the transcript raw |

## Vertical vs Horizontal Rules

This is the rule that keeps repurposing clean:

- **Long-form = horizontal.** Your core YouTube videos are shot and edited horizontal, for depth, screen-sharing, and watch-time.
- **Shorts / vertical = vertical.** Shorts, Reels, and TikTok cuts are vertical. Reframe properly — subject centred, captions burned in or added natively, key visuals repositioned for the vertical frame.
- **Never just crop lazily.** A horizontal video shrunk into a vertical frame with tiny, unreadable text is not a repurposed asset; it is a damaged one.
- **Never stretch vertical into long-form.** A vertical clip does not become a long-form video. If a short performs well, that is a signal to *script a proper long-form video* on the topic (see `analytics-loop.md`).

Practical extraction rules:
- Cut on complete thoughts — a cut starts and ends mid-idea only if the loop is intentional and the payoff is inside the cut
- Add captions to every vertical cut; many viewers watch muted
- The first second of a vertical cut is its hook — start on the strongest line, not on "in my full video I explained…"
- Each platform gets its native upload, at human pace — do not blast the same cut to every platform in the same minute

## How to Extract, Step by Step

Do this once per long video, ideally in one sitting right after publish (or from the script, before publish, for the text assets).

1. **Timestamp the moments**
   - While reviewing the final edit, note timestamps for: the strongest claim, the clearest tip, the best story beat, and the myth / mistake moment
   - These four moments usually yield 2–4 vertical cuts

2. **Pull the quotes**
   - Search the script for lines you underlined while writing — they are your quote posts
   - 1–3 quotes per video is plenty; more dilutes them

3. **Build the carousel from the outline**
   - You already have sections — convert, don't create
   - Cover slide: the promise. Content slides: one idea each. Final slide: summary + single next step

4. **Write the community post**
   - One question, one poll, or one "which of these..." prompt drawn from the video's topic
   - Keep it genuinely conversational — it is a community surface, not an ad slot

5. **Draft the blog outline**
   - Title (adapted from the video title for search), intro from the hook, headings from the sections, conclusion from the CTA
   - Note where the written version should go *deeper* than the video — examples, checklists, links

6. **Log everything**
   - In your content tracker: source video, each derived asset, its format, and its status (drafted / ready / published)
   - Derived assets are published at human pace, spaced out — a repurposing batch is a queue, not a dump

## Spacing the Outputs

Repurposing multiplies output, which makes pacing discipline more important, not less (see `README.md`):

- Spread the derived assets across the days following the long video — they extend its life instead of cannibalising its launch
- Vary the gaps between posts; keep occasional rest days with nothing published at all
- One asset per platform per pass — never post every derived asset everywhere at once
- Respect each platform's own rules and your account's standing on it. If a platform has restricted a feature (for example, links or DMs), repurposing does not route around the restriction — you adapt the asset (no link) or you hold it.

## What Not to Repurpose

- **Half-formed ideas** that were cut from the script for being weak — cutting-room material stays on the floor
- **Time-sensitive claims** (deadlines, prices, rules) into evergreen assets without re-verifying them first
- **Client or case details** into posts or carousels without the same permission/anonymisation you applied in the video
- **The same cut, unchanged, everywhere, repeatedly** — repurposing is reformatting an idea for each surface, not copy-pasting one file until the audience is tired of it

## Quick Reference

```
LONG VIDEO (horizontal)
├── 2-4 vertical cuts        -> Shorts / Reels / TikTok
├── 1-3 quote posts          -> X / Threads / LinkedIn / Facebook
├── 1 carousel               -> Instagram / LinkedIn
├── 1 community post         -> YouTube Community
└── 1 blog outline           -> Website / blog draft
```

One idea. One recording. Everywhere it belongs.
