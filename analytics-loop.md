# Analytics Loop

Publishing is not the end of a video's work. It is the start of the feedback loop that makes the next video better.

This system has two rhythms: a **~25-hour post check** on every video, and a **weekly review** of the channel as a whole. Both are human, manual reviews — you read the numbers, you decide, you act. No automated bulk changes.

## The ~25-Hour Post Check

Check every video roughly **25 hours after publish** — long enough for the first wave of impressions and views to settle, soon enough to still rescue a packaging problem.

### What to look at

| Signal | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Views vs. channel baseline | Is this video performing at, above, or below what *your* channel normally gets in its first day? Compare to your own recent videos, not to other channels. |
| Click-through rate (CTR) | Are people who see the title + thumbnail actually clicking? Low CTR with decent retention usually means a packaging problem, not a content problem. |
| Retention / average view duration | Where do viewers drop off? A sharp drop early points to a weak hook or a title that promised something different. A gradual slope is normal; cliffs are information. |
| Traffic source | Browse, suggested, search, or external? It tells you *how* the video is being discovered, and whether the packaging matches that surface. |
| Comments | What are viewers actually asking or correcting? Comments are your next video ideas and your accuracy check. |

Record the check in your content tracker: video, publish date, check date, the three headline signals (views vs. baseline, CTR, retention shape), and the decision you made.

### Decision rules

- **Performing at or above baseline:** leave it alone. Do not tinker with a working title or thumbnail. Log what worked — topic, hook, packaging — for the weekly review.
- **Low CTR, retention is fine once people click:** it is a packaging problem. See rescue moves below.
- **Decent CTR, retention falls off a cliff in the first 30–60 seconds:** the hook or the promise is the problem. A new title will not fix it — note the lesson for the next script, and consider whether the opening can be clarified with a pinned comment.
- **Near-zero views / no impressions:** do not panic-edit repeatedly. Check the basics once (video is public, title/thumbnail render correctly, no policy or visibility issue), make *one* considered packaging change if warranted, and let the weekly review judge it with more data.

### Rescue moves for underperformers

Rescue means fixing discoverability, not disguising a weak video.

1. **New title**
   - Go back to the candidate titles you wrote at packaging stage (`packaging-checklist.md`)
   - Sharpen clarity first (what exactly does the viewer get?), then curiosity
   - Change one thing at a time — if you change title and thumbnail together, you will not know which one worked

2. **New thumbnail**
   - Simplify: fewer elements, bigger text, one clearer focal point
   - Test readability at small size again before uploading the replacement
   - Keep brand consistency — a rescue thumbnail should still look like your channel

3. **Description and pinned comment tidy-up**
   - Clarify the first two lines if the video's actual strongest content differs from what you summarised
   - Pin a comment that adds the missing context or answers the question early viewers keep asking

4. **Give it time, then re-check**
   - After a rescue change, note the date of the change in your tracker and check again at the weekly review
   - One rescue pass per video is the discipline. Endless re-editing of metadata is not a strategy — the next video is.

> Never rescue by re-uploading the same video, buying views, or using engagement manipulation. Those risk the channel and teach you nothing.

## The Weekly Review

Once a week, step back from individual videos and review the channel.

### Agenda

1. **Review the week's ~25-hour checks together**
   - Which videos beat baseline, which matched it, which missed — and what pattern connects them? Topic, format (tutorial / myth-busting / case breakdown), title style, thumbnail style?

2. **CTR and retention patterns**
   - Which title formulas earned clicks this week?
   - Where did retention consistently drop — openings, mid-video, endings? Feed the fix into `scripting-system.md` habits (hooks, pattern interrupts, section order).

3. **Top comments and questions**
   - List the questions viewers asked that you have not answered yet. This is your highest-quality idea source — real demand, in the viewer's own words.

4. **Repurposing performance**
   - Which vertical cuts, quote posts, or carousels from this week's long videos performed best on their platforms? Strong short-form performance on a topic is a signal (see below).

5. **Decisions for next week**
   - Next scripting batch topics (informed by steps 1–4, not by hunches alone)
   - Any series to continue, pause, or retire
   - One — only one — packaging or scripting experiment to run next week, written down in advance

### Double down on winners

When a video clearly outperforms your baseline, do not just celebrate it — extend it:

- **Part 2 / follow-up:** answer the follow-up questions the comments are asking, or go one level deeper on the same topic
- **Series:** if the format and topic both worked, structure the follow-ups as a named series with consistent packaging, and link them together (end screens, cards, playlist — see `packaging-checklist.md`)
- **Repurpose harder:** a winner earns extra cuts, a carousel, and a fuller blog piece (see `repurposing-map.md`)
- **Revisit the script:** identify *why* it worked — hook phrasing, section order, a specific example — and write that lesson into your scripting notes so it is repeatable, not luck

The same logic applies in reverse: a topic or format that misses repeatedly, despite good packaging, gets retired or rested. The channel's time goes where the audience's attention already is.

## The Loop, Whole

```
Publish (human pace, varied gaps)
   -> ~25-hour check: views vs. baseline, CTR, retention
      -> rescue once if packaging is the problem (title / thumbnail)
   -> Weekly review: patterns, comments, repurposing signals
      -> double down on winners (Part 2, series)
      -> lessons feed the next scripting batch
   -> Publish...
```

## What to Track

Keep it simple — a single tracker (spreadsheet or document) with one row per video:

- Title, publish date, format, series (if any)
- ~25-hour check: views vs. baseline, CTR, retention note, rescue action + date (if any)
- Weekly review verdict: winner / baseline / miss, and the lesson in one line
- Follow-up created? (Part 2, series entry, extra repurposing)

If a metric is not changing a decision you make, stop tracking it. The loop exists to make the next video better — not to build a dashboard nobody acts on.
