# YouTube Automation Engine — Skill

Automate the system *around* the videos — research, scripting structure,
packaging, checklists, repurposing, and review. Never automate spam:
no bulk uploads, no templated low-effort content, no fake engagement.

## What it covers

- `scripting-system.md` — hook / promise / sections / pattern interrupts /
  CTA spine, plus Tutorial, Myth-Busting, and Case Breakdown templates
- `packaging-checklist.md` — title formulas, thumbnail rules, description
  template with chapters, tags / end screens / cards, full upload checklist
- `repurposing-map.md` — one horizontal long video → vertical cuts, quote
  posts, a carousel, a community post, and a blog outline
- `analytics-loop.md` — the ~25-hour post check and the weekly review

## The workflow — apply it in order

1. **Script first.** Hook in the first 30 seconds (problem + open loop),
   an explicit one-sentence promise, 3–5 named sections, planned pattern
   interrupts, one CTA only. Script hook and CTA word-for-word; write for
   the ear and read every draft aloud. Batch scripting on its own day —
   outline all videos first, then write them one at a time.
2. **Package before you record.** Draft 3–5 title candidates and the
   thumbnail concept while scripting. Title = curiosity + clarity,
   front-loaded. Thumbnail = 3 elements max, 2–4 words, readable at phone
   size, one focal point, no duplicated wording with the title.
3. **Publish at human pace.** One video at a time, manually — never
   platform-native schedulers or bulk-upload tools. Vary the gaps between
   uploads, keep occasional full rest days, and respect your daily cap.
   An order for a series is an *order*, never a speed instruction.
4. **Repurpose, don't re-record.** From one long video extract 2–4 vertical
   cuts (complete thoughts, captions on, strongest line first), 1–3 quote
   posts, 1 carousel from the section structure, 1 community post, and 1
   blog outline. Reframe vertical properly — never shrink horizontal into
   vertical. Space derived assets across following days.
5. **Check at ~25 hours.** Compare views to your own channel baseline,
   read CTR and retention shape, note traffic source and top comments.
   Low CTR + good retention = packaging problem → one rescue (new title
   *or* new thumbnail, one change at a time). Early retention cliff =
   hook/promise lesson for the next script, not a title fix. Log it all.
6. **Review weekly.** Patterns across the week's checks, winning title
   formulas, repeated retention drop points, unanswered viewer questions
   (= next video ideas), and repurposing winners. Double down on winners
   with a Part 2 or series; run exactly one packaging/scripting experiment
   next week.

## Golden rules — never break these

- Quality is the strategy; human review before every publish.
- Long-form is horizontal; short-form outputs are vertical. Shorts are a
  repurposing output, never a replacement for the long-form core.
- Never rescue by re-uploading, buying views, or engagement manipulation.
- Case breakdowns: anonymise or get permission; never invent figures.
- Protect the account — nothing here is worth a warning or restriction.
  If a platform has restricted a feature, adapt the asset; never route
  around the restriction.

## How to apply this when helping a user

- Identify their stage (idea / scripting / recorded / published / review)
  and work that stage's checklist, not the whole engine at once.
- When drafting, deliver the artefacts the files specify: a scripted hook,
  a promise sentence, title candidates, a thumbnail brief, a repurposing
  list with timestamps, or a 25-hour check verdict — concrete, not generic.
- Push back on bulk or scheduler-based plans and offer the paced version
  instead, using the README's framing.
